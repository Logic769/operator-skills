# Cold outreach sequence

Generic outbound gets ignored and hurts sender reputation. This skill writes fewer, shorter, more specific messages.

## Inputs to collect

Ask only for what is missing:

1. **Offer:** what you sell, in one sentence, and the outcome it creates.
2. **Proof:** one concrete result (a number, a named type of customer, a before and after).
3. **Target:** the person or segment, and why them now (the trigger).
4. **Ask:** the smallest next step (a reply, a 15-minute call, a free audit).
5. **Channel:** email, LinkedIn, or both.

## Writing rules

- Email 1 is under 90 words. Follow-ups are under 60.
- First line is about them, not you. It references the trigger, not flattery.
- One idea per message. One ask per message.
- No "I hope this finds you well", "quick question", "just following up", "circle back", or "touch base".
- No fake personalization. If there is no real trigger, say so and write a segment-level message instead.
- Subject lines are 2 to 4 words, lowercase, no clickbait.
- No links in email 1 (better deliverability). One link at most later.
- Plain English. No em dashes. No exclamation marks.

## Sequence shape

| Step | Day | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0 | Trigger, problem, proof, soft ask |
| 2 | 3 | New angle: a specific idea or mini-insight they can use even if they never reply |
| 3 | 8 | Short close: ask if it is a priority, offer to stop |

## Output format

```
Target: <who> | Trigger: <why now> | Ask: <next step>

Email 1, day 0
Subject: ...
Body: ...

Email 2, day 3
...

Email 3, day 8
...

LinkedIn connection note (under 200 characters): ...
```

After the sequence, list any claim you made that the user must verify (numbers, customer names) before sending.
